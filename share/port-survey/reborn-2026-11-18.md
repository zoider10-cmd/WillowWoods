# Reborn: the hitch fixes ported (2026-11-18)

- Item 3, a GPU unswizzle reads only a pool already imported: PORTED (8398466b, on; RX_VK_UNSWIZZLE_IMPORT=1 is the old
  way). A/B moving Market -> Kakariko -> Kokiri Forest -> Hyrule Field, 4 pairs: frame-blocking imports per run ~39 -> 23
  (the 10 ms+ ones 8-10 -> 8), fps 125.2 both, 1% low 58.0 -> 59.1 (11 of 16 pairs, NOISE by the gate). Kept: nothing worse,
  fewer stalls. The worst frames (~140 ms) are the teleports' loads, which it does not touch.
- Item 2, import fence and commit marks: NOT PORTED. Mako's RX_VK_IMPORT_SPLIT already makes an import's range a region of
  its own; our logs give an import's "commit+properties" (the walk) 0.5 ms. The 10-14 ms left is vkAllocateMemory itself
  (the AMD driver locking the pages), which neither touches.
- Descriptor reuse (Triforce, RX_VK_DESC_KEEP) A/B in the Market, 5 pairs: 74% of descriptors reused, fps 98.7 -> 99.0,
  1% low 59.3 -> 59.0: NOISE, stays off.

## The game's memory (2026-11-18)
- Measured from outside in the Market (headless): 17.9-22 GB resident: the game's window 9.4 GB (heap 6.9 GB + mapped
  pages 4.9 GB, against the console's 9.8 GB total), ~7 GB of the renderer's and driver's 16-64 MB blocks, 0.8 GB a mapped
  shader cache (.parc). The system's free RAM fell to ~0.5-0.6 GB and Windows trimmed the game from 22 to 13.6 GB.
- HOS_MEMINFO_NEW=1 (the game told what it uses; off since Mako 4.1.1): Market, 4 pairs: mapped 4.9 -> 2.8 GB, fps
  99.3 -> 100.1, 1% low 60.3 -> 60.6 (NOISE). Area route (Market, Kakariko, Kokiri, Hyrule Field), 3 pairs: mapped 6.7 ->
  2.8 GB, fps 124.7 -> 132.0 (+5.9%), 1% low 58.6 -> 53.7 (-8.3%, 9 of 9 pairs): FAIL. Kept off: it caches less and
  reloads at area entries. A middle figure (more room than the console's, less than none) is untried.
- Also ruled out in the Market: descriptor reuse, short-sleep pollers (4/8/all). Profile and chain: the frame is the
  game's own CPU work across main + ~11 job threads.

## What the "renderer's 7 GB" is (2026-11-18, Market, headless, from outside: QueryWorkingSet + reading the blocks)
- 16.2 GB resident: the game window 9.3 GB; 357 private 16 MB blocks 5.2 GB; the shader cache (.parc) 0.73 GB; a 258 MB and
  a 128 MB block (render/staging); exe and DLLs 0.25 GB. Vulkan's own system-RAM heap is only ~0.46 GB (its blocks).
- The 16 MB blocks are Windows' segment heap (malloc's large segments: heads 0xddeeddee / 0xddccddcc), almost all filled by
  AMD's driver with shader code:
  - SPIR-V: 29,048 modules, 2.19 GB, only 14,809 distinct: each program's code twice, held by its VkShaderModule. Our own
    code drops its copy after vkCreateShaderModule.
  - AMD pipeline binaries (ELF, gfx1201): 51,590, 1.80 GB, 33,273 distinct: the live pipelines' plus the pipeline caches'
    entries (hookshot_vk_pipelines.bin is 1.19 GB, every area ever played, loaded whole at start).
- Letting the modules go: vk_draw.cpp pipeline_cache_play() already does it, but only on Linux (<=20 GB RAM) and only for
  programs precompile_run built. Here RX_VK_LOAD=create builds nothing before play ("none built"), so it would let go nothing;
  tried on Windows with RX_VK_DROP_MODULES=1: 0 dropped, reverted. Doing it in play needs a guard against freeing a module
  a pipeline worker is building from (a crash), so not tried.
- Untested: whether low free RAM costs the 1% low at all (HOS_MEMINFO_NEW freed 2-4 GB with no Market gain).
- Does low free RAM cost the 1% low? Market A/B, 3 pairs (the first pair's runs lost to the script), another program
  holding 5.2 GB and touching it each second: free RAM ~5.8-6.7 GB -> ~0.8-2.5 GB. fps 98.7 -> 109.6, 1% low 58.9 -> 59.9
  (gate PASS: no worse). But two of the three held runs had one very slow frame (294 and 215 ms against 27-48 ms): Windows
  paging the game back in. So short RAM does not lower the 1% low here; it can add rare long hitches. Freeing the driver's
  shader copies is not worth its crash risk for the 1% low; it might be for those hitches on a busier PC.
