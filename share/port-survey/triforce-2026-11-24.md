## Notes

### 1. GPU timestamps through a ring, not vkGetQueryPoolResults (RX_VK_QUERY_RING, on) - PORT FIRST
- **What it does.** Each recording slot's next use starts with `vkCmdCopyQueryPoolResults` of its last use's timestamps
  (value plus availability word, `WITH_AVAILABILITY`, no `WAIT_BIT`) into a host-coherent mapped buffer, then a
  COPY->HOST barrier, then the reset as before. The slot's following turn reads the words from memory, so GPU time arrives
  one turn of the 48-slot ring later (about 2 frames). It falls back to vkGetQueryPoolResults without host-coherent memory
  or with `=0`. A use that was never submitted is not read (`r.submitted`).
- **Measured (theirs).** RX 6700 XT, AMD Windows: about 0.6 ms a frame on the replay thread, 7.7 % of its running time
  (two calls a recording, about 25 recordings a frame). With `RX_VK_RECORDER=thread`, each call was also a boundary that
  waited for the recorder.
- **Ours.** We don't have it. `gpu_time_collect` (vk_replay.cpp:393) makes one vkGetQueryPoolResults call a recording (we
  have no `ts_up_pool`), so expect about half their figure on AMD Windows. We have the recorder thread, and
  `vkCmdCopyQueryPoolResults` is already in vk_recorder.h's list and vk_work.h (line 91). We already use the same
  copy-to-host-buffer pattern for occlusion counts (`occ_host_buf`, vk_replay.cpp:785). It feeds `perf_monitor_note_gpu_time`
  and the FPS counter's GPU time, which average over many frames, so the 2-frame delay doesn't matter.
- **Port.** Mechanical: `query_ring_make`, the copy and barrier in `gpu_time_begin`, and the ring read in
  `gpu_time_collect` (our `t[2]`, no `ts_count`/sim_collect). Add a switches.def row (kill, on) and port
  tests/test_query_ring.py with its upload-pool check removed. Confirm `Recording` has a `submitted` flag, or add one.
- **Risk.** Low: GPU time readouts only. Check that the overlay's GPU ms matches the old path (RX_VK_QUERY_RING=0) in a
  Market A/B.

### 2. Resolution Auto that steps down on a slow card (Triforce 11-17 RX_VK_DYNRES_GPU, plus 11-23 union and GPU-limited gate) - PORT, gate with an A/B
- **The base (11-17, in v0.7.0, never listed in our surveys).** The game's dynamic resolution reads a frame's GPU time from
  two timestamp reports. We write those as the real clock since the present times `RX_VK_TS_SCALE`, a fixed 0.1
  (vk_replay.cpp:1532; design.md rule 4). Triforce found that this keeps the measured time under 1 ms against the game's
  ~16.4 ms threshold, so **Auto stays at its top level on every card**. Their clock (`ts_dyn_present`) sets the scale to
  the recordings' measured GPU time over the replay's wall span between the frame's first and last reports, each averaged
  over ~30 presents and clamped to 0.02-8. A card that can't draw the frame in time then gets the 3D stepped down, as on
  the console.
- **The 11-23 fixes.**
  - **(a) Union.** On AMD's Windows driver a recording's first timestamp is written while the previous recording still
    runs, so summing intervals counted overlaps twice: 14.9 ms against a 7.8 ms span on an RX 6700 XT at 1440p, and Auto
    dropped to 854x480 with the card half idle. `gpu_busy.h` (35 lines, pure) counts each moment once.
  - **(b) Gate.** A recording that waits on the GPU for a CPU-set event counts that wait as GPU time (19.8 ms vs a 5.9 ms
    span). So the scale is capped at 1.0 unless the host waits on the GPU for 2 ms a frame or more, released under 1 ms.
    The waits are the slot waits plus every fence and sync-point wait (`gpu_wait_kinds.h`, compositor excluded).
- **Ours.** We have none of the three parts. Our default Render resolution is Auto (`opts.resolution = 0`, vk_menu.h:31), so
  on a Steam Deck, an iGPU or a weak dGPU the game never lowers its 3D size and the frame rate takes the hit.
- **Hardware.** Steam Deck, iGPUs, GTX 10xx / RX 5xx-class cards, any card at 4K. On the owner's 9070 XT the gate keeps
  the scale at or under 1 while the replay, not the card, is the limit, so the 3D size should stay at the top. It changes
  only where the card is the limit (a 4K + FSR 4 case to check).
- **Port.**
  - **Pieces:** take 11-17's `ts_first_ns`/`ts_last_ns` capture in the counter report, `ts_dyn_present` at the present
    anchor and `scale_now`, together with 11-23's union and gate. Do not port the 11-17 sum alone: on AMD it steps the 3D
    down wrongly.
  - **gpu_wait_kinds.h:** we lack it (86 lines plus about 16 instrumented wait sites). Taking its fence/sync-point counting
    is the bulk of the work. A smaller first step is the gate on our `st_wait_ns` plus the frame fence wait, but Triforce
    showed slot waits alone missed a GPU limit (0.03 ms a frame while the host waited 6-17 ms).
  - **Also:** update design.md rule 4 (a `doc:` anchor sits on that code: run tests/test_doc_anchors.py), add switches.def
    rows (RX_VK_DYNRES_GPU, RX_VK_DYNRES_UNION), and port tests/test_gpu_busy.py.
- **Risk.** Medium. A wrong clock makes the picture soft (too low) or does nothing (too high). Each part has an `=0` kill.
  Give it a Market A/B on the owner's PC (render size over time from the log's "dynamic resolution's clock" line, no 3D
  size change at 1440p), and put "Steam Deck: Auto steps down and recovers" on the verify list.

### 3. File handles at the commit limit (nn_fs.c) - PORT (tiny)
- **What it does.** Triforce ticket 1574276312678408316: at Windows' commit limit the OpenFile handle's `calloc` returned
  NULL and the next write crashed the game as "write 0" with no word of memory. Now the handle, the directory handle and
  the mod-extras array use the waiting allocators, which stop the game with a reason if still refused. It also logs (first
  8) a file of a writable mount that is there but would not open; on Linux that state left the save manager looping with
  no file select.
- **Ours.** We have the same bare `calloc` at nn_fs.c:436 and 683, and `realloc` at 674. Our `hos_xcalloc`/`hos_xrealloc`
  (mkw_glue.c:149, waits at the commit limit, then `hos_fatal` with the reason) take a `what` argument, so this is three
  one-line swaps plus the log (capture `GetLastError()` before `hos_vcore_back`).
- **Hardware.** 16 GB PCs with a small or fixed page file; Linux saves with bad permissions (the log line).

### 4. The image wait off the frame's path on AMD Windows (RX_VK_ACQUIRE_AHEAD, Triforce's form) - LATER, measure first
- **What it does.** On AMD Windows (DXGI presentation) an acquired image comes back at the compositor's next refresh.
  Triforce's present thread now acquires *and waits on the fence* (up to 250 ms) right after its present. `present_image`
  then finds the image `held.free` and records its blit at once. On AMD Windows it turns the present thread on for this
  (`present_thread_wanted`), never with DLSS-FG, and never while a resize or a lost surface is pending.
- **Measured (theirs).** RX 6700 XT, 26.11.2, game-bound at 45-52 fps: 2.4-4.2 ms of every frame in that wait, 16 % of the
  presenting thread.
- **Ours.** We have Longshot's acquire ahead (12bd87e0, `acquire_ahead`, vk_replay.cpp:5744-5800), on by default, but only
  where the presenter thread runs. Our `RX_VK_PRESENT_THREAD` is **off on every PC** ("until the swapchain's external sync
  and the drain are fixed"), and our ahead image is waited for at its present, not on the presenter. So on the owner's PC
  that wait is still on the replay's thread.
- **Before porting.** Measure whether the owner's PC has this wait: a run with `RX_VK_ACQUIRE_AHEAD=1` logs the image-wait
  counter every 600 presents. If it is in the ms range, the port is to move the fence wait into our `acquire_ahead` and get
  the presenter thread safe enough to turn on for AMD Windows. That is the real work, not Triforce's 60 lines.
- **Risk.** Medium-high (present path, swapchain sync). This item could help the owner's own card the most, so measuring it
  is worth a slot.

### 5. One L3 for the frame's two hot threads on two-die Ryzen 9 (HOS_L3_HOME, on, Windows) - PORT LATER (no effect on the owner)
- **What it does.** On a CPU with 2 or more equal-size L3s and 6 or more cores under the first, the replay's reserved core
  becomes the first die's last core (it was the CPU's last, on die 2). The game's main thread and "Presentation Thread" are
  kept on die 1's other cores; everything else is left alone (NX2navi found pinning the whole game to 6 cores loses).
  Nothing changes on one-L3 CPUs, X3D two-size parts, 3900X-style 3-core CCXs, or multi-group systems.
- **Measured (theirs).** 5900X + RX 6700 XT: 12 ms of replay a frame in play before. No after figure is given.
- **Ours.** We have our own X3D rule (`pick_cache_cores`, HOS_CACHE_CORES, hos_thread.c:747-860) and `pick_reserved_core` /
  `last_fastest_core`, but no equal-L3 home. The port is `hos_l3_home.h` with its `HosCache` type reshaped to our cache
  listing, a mask test in `last_fastest_core`, and the pin in `hos_thread_pin` (our thread names: check that "Presentation
  Thread" and sead::MainThread are the names our pins see). Port tests/test_l3_home.py too.
- **Hardware.** 12/16-core Ryzen 9 desktops. None on the owner's single-die CPU (per the 11-22 survey). We can't test it
  here, so it goes on the verify list.

### 6. Render-target pool cap also for wide pictures (TEX-85) - PORT (tiny)
- Our `hos_rt_pool_size` (nn_applet.c:194) caps at 900 MB only when the *output* is over 3840x2160 (`big`). A 4K output
  on a 21:9 screen makes a 5120x2160 picture (our `hos_wide_max_px` default) and scales to 915 MB, uncapped. 1000 MB and
  up are known to fail the game's reservation at boot; 915 is untested. Triforce caps on the wide picture's pixels too.
- Port: `big` from `max(output px, wide px)`, plus their four test rows in tests/test_rt_pool_minimum.py. It only lowers
  a pool by 15 MB in that one case.

### 7. A one-core waiter does not escape past a better holder (HOS_VCORE_PIN_ESCAPE_MS) - PORT, adapted
- **What it does.** Their 0.7.0 load crash (f_80119570, an effect graph half made) came every time right after
  PreActorModelCompose escaped core 1 or 2 while BaseProcCreateAndDeleteThread held it, so the two ran at once on one
  guest core. Now a waiter whose allowed mask is a single core, with a better-priority holder on it, waits up to 5 s, not
  the equal-priority escape time. A count goes in the 5 s vcore stats.
- **Ours.** Different numbers. Our `esc_eq` and `esc_better` are 2 s (theirs 250 ms), and after 1 s our starve rule
  (`HOS_VCORE_STARVE_US`) asks the worst holder to yield whatever its priority. So the crash window is narrower here but
  not closed: a 2 s escape on a single-core mask still lets two threads overlap. It happens most on slow CPUs during
  loads.
- **Port.** About 20 lines in `vc_acquire` (hos_thread.c:1840): the single-core test on `m` and `vc_owner[k]`, the longer
  limit, and the stat. Check first whether the owner's logs ever show "vcores: ... escape" lines. If they never do, this is
  only insurance for weaker PCs.

### 8-10. Small ones

  a choice.
- **9. Linux log file.** Our hos_main.c:5667 writes the log file only when stderr is none, so a Steam or terminal start on

- **10. Ultrawide menu sides' feather.** Our fg_scale.comp:115-116 is their old code. The feather now lies inside
  [x0, x1], not outside it. Regenerate fg_scale_comp_spv.h with glslang. Visual only, ultrawide only, low value.
