## Ranked candidates

### 1. The game's texture heap compacted at most once a second (7b229f6, #1478) - PORT FIRST

- What: the game's texture data manager (f_80797240) asks its Compaction thread for a round at **every frame**; each
  texture a round moves reaches the renderer as a brand-new texture (ASTC decode, upload, descriptors, memory sync). In
  the Market the heap sits at its 5% free line, so batches of ~23 textures (10 MB) arrive every 4-14 frames: 3-5 ms
  each. A lift-site patch lets the request through at most once a second (HOS_TEX_COMPACT_MS, default 1000; 0 = the
  game's own every frame; off = never).
- Measured: Windows 11, RTX 4070 SUPER, Ryzen 9 9950X3D, standing in the Market, two runs a side: frames over 16.7 ms
  5.4 / 12.6% -> 1.0 / 1.5%; fps 70.8 / 67.2 -> 75.4 / 74.9; p99 18.5 / 23.5 -> 16.7 / 17.0 ms; worst 31 / 221 -> 20.5 / 21 ms;
  **memory sync 0.63 / 0.79 -> 0.38 / 0.39 ms a frame**; ASTC textures made again 1,185 / 2,489 -> 221 / 275.
- Ours: missing (no patch at f_80797240, no host/oot_tex_compact.*).
- Hardware: none; game-side. Should help any PC, most of all one whose Market frame is replay/upload-bound (ours). Their
  open question: what the game does when a large texture does not fit because compaction ran later (not seen).
- Default on (1000 ms); HOS_TEX_COMPACT_MS=0 is the A/B's "before" with one build.
- Size: ~300 lines, of which host/oot_tex_compact.c/.h ~95, nx2/oot_patches.py +41 (a post-lift patch of the generated C,
  like our other oot_patches; no re-lift of nx2/ code generation), gpu/vk/switches.def row (we have no switches.def:
  drop it), a test.
- Randomizer: **touches nx2/oot_patches.py and adds a host/oot_* file**. The patch is a separate function in patch_file;
  check that every randomizer patch still applies and the rando guard passes.
- Risk: medium (the game's heap tidied less often than it expects). Easy to A/B with HOS_TEX_COMPACT_MS=0.
- Why first: the only commit in the range aimed squarely at our Market symptom (memory sync, texture re-creation
  bursts, the "laggy start" pattern), measured on Windows, with a one-switch A/B.

### 5. Replay CPU cuts from the renderer rework (347aa6e, #1484, parts 1, 6, 7) - PORT FIRST (A/B in the Market and Kakariko)

  table walk in dead_targets_sweep).
- Hardware: their worry is picture bugs at part ends on other drivers (Intel history) and slower CPUs waiting longer for

- Size: the three pieces ~150 lines (vk_replay.cpp flush_ub_updates + UbBatch; vk_memory.cpp forget; vk_texture.cpp

### 8. The swapchain image waited for on the CPU: no GPU hang with the fullscreen window behind another (7f880b3, #1458) - PORT FIRST if it reproduces

  any GPU: a hidden window now drops frames instead of blocking.
- Size: ~90 lines vk_replay.cpp present_image (+ tests). No randomizer file. Risk: medium (present path).

### 10. The vsync ticker waits on a high-resolution timer instead of yielding (e9478ed, #1235) - TRY THE SWITCH FIRST

- What: vsync_main's wait goes through hos_sleep_ns, which on Windows yields in a loop for any wait under 1 ms (host
  sleeps are not covered by our short-sleep timer rule). Uncapped, the ticker's period is 1 ms: a whole core spun.
  Fix: a per-thread CreateWaitableTimerExW high-resolution timer for the ticker only (HOS_VSYNC_YIELD=1: old wait).
- Measured: Linux, Ryzen 7 5800X, **RX 9070 XT** (RADV), Hyrule Field route at 1440p, 3 interleaved runs a side:
  median frame -4.3% and -5.2% on the moving legs (beyond noise), load leg and 800p within noise; ticker CPU 99.9% ->
  1.9% of a core. Windows not run.
- Ours: missing (nn_vi.c vsync_main -> hos_sleep_ns -> yield loop for host sleeps < 1 ms).
- **No-code test first**: `HOS_SHORT_SLEEP_TIMER=1` puts all short sleeps (host's too) on the timer. If that helps in
  the Market/Kakariko uncapped, port the 30-line ticker-only change (narrower than the global switch).
- Hardware: matters most when uncapped or with a high cap, and on CPUs with few cores. Risk: low (falls back to yield).
