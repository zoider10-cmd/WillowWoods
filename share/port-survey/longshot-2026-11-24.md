## Ranked candidates

### 1. A CPU read waits only for GPU writes to its own bytes (682a6640, #2366; RX_VK_WB_SPANS, on) - PORT FIRST
- **What it does.** The write-back notes are kept per 4 KB page. OoT's four double-buffered culling tables (0x9b040,
  0x44b040, 0x8b040 and 0x1b580 bytes) put both slots on one shared page. So the CPU's read of last frame's slot waited
  for this frame's GPU write of the other slot. The fix keeps, for each page that a write covers only in part, the bytes
  it covered and the page's earlier state (`wb_ext`, `wb_rest_v`, `wb_rest_p`). A read waits for the last write only if
  that write touched its bytes. It never waits longer than before.
- **Measured.** RTX 4070 SUPER, Windows, 4K DLAA, Castle Town, two interleaved runs each:
  - game_ms p99: 17.6 -> 13.7 ms
  - p99 frame: 23.3 -> 21.6 ms
  - 1% low: 26.3 -> 24.7 ms
  - fps: +1.5 %
  - frames over 20 ms: 170 -> 74
  It equals the old player workaround `RX_VK_READBACK_LATE=3980,9b040,8b040,44b040,1b580`, but exactly, without reading
  stale bytes.
- **Our tree.** Same code. `wb_pages_note` and `memory_writeback_value` (vk_memory.cpp ~1785-1830) match their "before"
  hunk nearly line for line. We have two extras: `wb_direct` and the `RX_VK_NO_READBACK_WAIT` early-out. Our
  READBACK_LATE default is `3980,40,2000`, which leaves out the four table sizes, so these stalls very likely happen
  here. We don't have it.
- **Hardware.** Every GPU that runs behind: low-end cards at 720p/1080p, Steam Deck, iGPUs, and any card at 4K. It
  helps the owner wherever the 9070 XT is GPU-bound. It cannot hurt AMD, low VRAM or a weak CPU: a few page-table
  lookups under a lock that is already taken.
- **Port.** About 110 lines in vk_memory.cpp, a switches.def row (kill, on), and their `tests/test_wb_spans.py`. Small
  edits to our `test_readback_part.py` and `test_late_fix_build.py`. `env_on_or` doesn't exist here, so read the switch
  with `getenv` + `env_value_off`.
- **Risk.** Low. It is never stricter than before. Their test checks the rule against a per-byte model over 2,000 random
  cases. Off switch: `RX_VK_WB_SPANS=0`.

### 2. Handle tables end at the game's count (b9bd74a2, #2258; RX_VK_HANDLE_TABLE_COUNT, on) - PORT FIRST (unchanged from 11-22)
- **Still not in our tree.** There is no `handle_table_live` or `RX_VK_HANDLE_TABLE_COUNT`, and `stage_handles` still
  fills all 64 elements.
- **Measured.** RTX 4070 SUPER, 4K, after the pause menu: 6.4 / 10.7 GB re-uploaded -> 12 / 117 MB; frames over
  16.7 ms 15 / 45 % -> 0.8 / 2.0 %. On an RX 580 (RADV), "uploaded again" lines went 40 -> 2.
- **Hardware.** All GPUs. The most gain is on low VRAM and narrow PCIe, where re-uploads cost the most.
- **Port.** About 100 lines plus the test, as the 11-22 report describes: `SimTurfStorageOrig(d)` becomes our
  `d.cbuf_offset`.
- **Risk.** Low to medium: a missing light or shadow if the layout check is fooled. Off switch:
  `RX_VK_HANDLE_TABLE_COUNT=0`.

### 3. Exact handle staleness, blocks-hash memo, zero depth clears (59f9632b, #2343, three parts of #2273) - PORT FIRST/EARLY
- **What it does.** Three parts:
  - **(a) RX_VK_EXACT_STALE.** A small log records which texture each destroy generation removed (`gone_note`), and
    `texture_still_resolved` keeps a handle whose own texture didn't go. Before, one global `destroy_gen` made every
    resolved handle stale.
  - **(b) RX_VK_HASH_MEMO.** The ASTC reuse blocks hash is remembered per storage address and shape while
    `memory_written_since` says the bytes stand.
  - **(c) RX_VK_DEPTH_ZERO.** A depth texture read before the GPU drew it, with all-zero storage (the shadow maps drawn
    at 816 and sampled at 812/813), gets a GPU clear instead of a CPU unswizzle. Before, `upload_all_zero` handled this
    only for storage of 32 MB and more.
- **Measured.** Steam Deck LCD under forced VRAM churn (`RX_VK_COMPACT_PCT=60`), all three on against all three off:
  - frames over 60 ms: 263 -> 146
  - staged texture data: 3,067 -> 1,617 MB
  Their note on pause-menu close: 11 ms of lookups and 12 ms of descriptor builds in the next frame. The paired
  measurements at the branch head were still pending in the PR.
- **Our tree.** All three bases exist:
  - one global `destroy_gen` (vk_texture.cpp:2216; bumped at 2219, 4633, 4757)
  - `HandleHit` with `destroy_gen` (vk_draw.cpp:3497)
  - `Texture::id` (vk_texture.h:89)
  - `blocks_hash` and `reuse_upload` (vk_texture.cpp ~1700-1720)
  - `upload_all_zero` (1751)
  - `memory_written_since` and `memory_generation` (vk_internal.h:413-415)
  We don't have any of the three parts.
- **Hardware.** Steam Deck, low VRAM, iGPUs, narrow PCIe (fewer uploads and CPU unswizzles). It should be neutral or
  positive on the 9070 XT.
- **Port.** About 125 lines in vk_texture.cpp/.h and vk_draw.cpp, plus 3 switches.def rows. Small adaptations:
  - our `memory_generation()` returns uint32
  - our `destroy_later` takes no `completed`
  - our compaction/placeholder bump sites are at different lines
  Check that `desc_keep.h` and the descriptor-reuse paths that read `texture_destroy_generation()` (vk_draw.cpp 4293,
  5323) still behave the same. They keep the global counter, as in Longshot.
- **Risk.** Low to medium: a stale texture surviving a destroy would show as a wrong-texture frame. Each part has its
  own kill switch (=0). Test pause open/close, area changes and a VRAM-churn run.

### 4. Intel EQUAL-depth margin: off on Lunar Lake (641030ed, #2299), plus the older rules we lack - PORT (tiny, Intel-only)
- **What it does.** The margin that turns EQUAL into LESS_OR_EQUAL lets foliage cut-outs draw as solid quads. 641030ed
  leaves it off on Lunar Lake (`0x64xx`).
- **Our tree is older than all of Longshot's rule.** vk_draw.cpp:5702 sets
  `vendorID == 0x8086 ? 64 : 0`: the margin on *every* Intel GPU and driver. That includes:
  - Mesa/Linux, which Longshot measured without holes and leaves at 0
  - Battlemage 0xE2xx (#1801/#1413: opaque rectangles on leaves, Link's hair, tree cards)
  - Lunar Lake
  We also lack Longshot's "blending pass keeps EQUAL" exception (`bool blends` loop, Longshot vk_draw.cpp ~6176-6182),
  which stops a khaki veil on Death Mountain Trail.
- **Port.** About 15 lines: `equal_depth_bias(driver, device)` (Intel Windows driver only; 0 on E2xx and 64xx), the
  blend check, the switch row text, and our `test_equal_depth_margin.py`.
- **Hardware.** Intel only (Arc B-series, Lunar Lake, Linux Intel). There is no effect on AMD or NVIDIA: the vendor test
  stays. `RX_VK_EQ_BIAS` still overrides it.
- **Risk.** Low. Possible holes on an untested Intel driver/area, fixed by `RX_VK_EQ_BIAS=64`. We can't test it here
  (no Intel GPU), so the Intel rows should go on the verify list.

### 5. A "lacked" load marker is not followed on PCs with 20 GB of RAM or less (b654a23a, #2256) - PORT
- **What it does.** A settings change and restart can write a load marker. On a 16 GB PC, start mode then loads every
  listed program before the title on every launch: an Arc A750 / 16 GB machine used 25.8 GB and sat for 10+ minutes.
  The marker never expires. The fix:
  - `marker_kind` tells the two writers apart ("waits" vs "lacked")
  - a "lacked" marker is ignored at 20 GB of RAM or less (`kStartRamMb`, the same line as `kSmallRamMb`)
  - a "waits" marker and RAM not known count as before
  - `RX_VK_LOAD` still wins
- **Our tree.** Same writers: `marker_write` and `marker_write_lacked` (load_default.h:73/96), `programs_load_decide`
  (vk_programs.cpp:347), and `kSmallRamMb` in pcache_load.h. We don't have it.
- **Hardware.** 16 GB PCs, any GPU. No change above 20 GB. If the owner's PC has 20 GB or less, it changes only the
  lacked-marker case.
- **Port.** About 80 lines plus their test. Small risk.

### 6. Build robustness on players' PCs: zero bytes and changed translations (38500511 #2300; 57ec7dad #2305) - PORT (Python)
- **What it does.**
  - 38500511: after the lift, `make_game.py` also scans the translation for zero bytes (we check only the last two
    bytes, `lifted_cut_short`, make_game.py:2206). If found, it translates again once, then stops with a count and
    "check memory/drive".
  - 57ec7dad: when every compile failure is in a translated file, the build translates again and compares. If files
    differ, the PC changed them: the build names them and recompiles once. Otherwise it stops with a memory/CPU message
    instead of a compiler error.
- **Measured.** The check costs 0.2-0.3 s of a 100 s translation. Nothing else runs on a good build.
- **Hardware.** Overclocked or flaky-RAM PCs. That matters because Willow Woods users build on their own PCs.
- **Port.** About 60 + 95 lines in nx2/make_game.py (+ lift_store.py), plus two tests. Our make_game.py has diverged
  (Mako + our additions), so expect hand merging around `translate()` and the compile `run()`. It doesn't touch
  oot_patches.py or game behaviour.
- **Risk.** Low.

### 7. GPU ASTC decoder exactly as astcenc (94cd0a20, #2314) - LATER (changes the owner's picture slightly)
- **What it does.**
  - stores through an `R8G8B8A8_UINT` view (truncation, as astcenc does)
  - adds an sRGB decode mode as a push constant
  - fixes four decode faults: 4/5-bit weight unquantize, the partial trit/quint group reading spare bits, illegal blocks
    decoded as legal, and HDR endpoints made magenta
  - drops about 400 lines of the shader
- **Measured.** Perf within 3 % on an RTX; 5-8 % faster on an AMD iGPU. In-game texel comparison: 82 % equal before ->
  100 %. Largest error before: 113/255, on 0.19 % of texels.
- **Our tree.** We have 658e5f2b's decoder (d32dd348) plus our own 6e11217e (bounds-checked stores from Fritchii). It
  still uses the `rgba8` UNORM store and `/65535`. We don't have it.
- **Hardware.** Every GPU on the GPU ASTC route, including the owner's 9070 XT. About 18 % of texels move by one step,
  and rare wrong blocks are fixed. Mostly invisible.
- **Port.** Medium:
  - shader hunks (conflict with our 6e11217e stores)
  - regenerate `astc_decoder_comp_spv.h` with glslang
  - UINT views in vk_texture.cpp (`astc_target_view`, `astc_level_view`, `bc_scratch_make` MUTABLE_FORMAT twins)
  - three tests to update
  Their test uses 3e75d518's texdecode as its reference. The shader itself doesn't need it.
- **Risk.** Medium: the format and view changes on AMD's Windows driver, which they did not test. A/B on the owner's PC
  is required.

- **Hardware.** AMD desktop iGPUs (load times and hitches) and older CPUs. No effect on the owner's default route (GPU

- **Port.** Large: about 2,150 lines, mostly the library and tests. Changes:
