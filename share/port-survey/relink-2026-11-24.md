# ProjectReLink survey, 2026-11-24 (34efe843..origin/HEAD 54af18c "Version 0.7.9.4", 3 commits; read only)

**Goal (the owner's, 2026-11-24): compatibility and performance across all PCs and platforms** (NVIDIA, Intel, AMD, low
VRAM, narrow PCIe, Steam Deck/Linux, older CPUs, 16 GB RAM), never breaking the owner's PC (RX 9070 XT, Windows 11).

Read with `git show` in the no-checkout clone (squashed history: the three commits' diffs). Nothing of theirs built, run
or installed. Our tree searched at C:\Users\beerc\Hookshot (amd-fix-sharkiix-version).

The range is two content commits and a version bump:
- **550bde6** (host): XInput on its own thread, the virtual core given up during texel reads/writes, OptimizeChangeBank's
  "neighbouring area" hold, HOS_SHADOW_BUCKET_ALL (opt-in).
- **88e38c0** (Vulkan, +956/-80 in 11 files): pipelines made ahead turned off again, programs read ahead at set,
  compute modules let go, a faster no-mesh fallback, GPL rework (opt-in), indirect mesh draws converted at the game's
  barriers, parts ending at the game's pass end, the reaper giving submits/presents the driver first, capture buffers
  freed on the reaper, ASTC->BC3 4 layers a run on NVIDIA, diagnostics.
- **54af18c**: launcher version only.

**Summary.** Our renderer is far apart from theirs in the program/pipeline area: we never made graphics pipelines ahead
(their headline change is turning theirs off), we already load kept programs at their create calls (RX_VK_LOAD=create,
Mako's design, which does what their "programs read ahead" does), we already let go of every ready program's modules
after the precompile, and our compute fallback already dispatches only the slots a task started and draws only the
records written. What is new and lands cleanly is in the replay, where our code is still the same as theirs: indirect
mesh draws converted once per barrier stretch, and a part's end moved to the game's own pass end (+3.1 % standing,
together). These help every GPU on the native mesh path, the owner's included. Behind them come three small items: the
reaper letting the driver's submits/presents go first and taking capture-buffer frees (submits held over 1 ms 66 -> 37),
ASTC->BC3 decoding 4 layers a run on NVIDIA (-21 % GPU a decode), and XInput polled on its own thread (helps keyboard
players on PCs with Bluetooth or virtual-pad drivers). The OptimizeChangeBank hold is the most interesting CPU item
(Market edge: area reloads -73 %), but it is a lift patch in nx2/oot_patches.py, so it is **not to be ported** under the
survey's rule.

## Candidates

### 1. Indirect mesh draws converted at the game's barriers (88e38c0, RX_VK_INDIRECT_AHEAD, on) - PORT FIRST, behind the Market A/B
- **What it does.** NVN's indirect mesh draws hold {count, first}; Vulkan reads {x, y, z}. Both trees copy each draw's
  counts into a ring as {count, 1, 1} right before the draw (`mesh_indirect_convert`): end the render pass, barrier,
  fill, barrier, copy, barrier. Every foliage draw paid a pass end and three barriers. Now at each NOP_BARRIER the
  replay scans forward to the next op that may write memory (a barrier, dispatch, copy, update, event, the stream's
  end; state binds, clears and plain draws don't stop it), collects the NOP_DRAW_MESH ops with an indirect buffer, and
  converts them all at once right after the game's barrier (`mesh_convert_ahead`). Each draw then finds its records by
  its op pointer and a generation (`mesh_op_set`, `mesh_ahead`). The counts are written by the GPU's culling before
  the game's barrier, the order the game itself relies on.
- **Measured (theirs).** "+3.1 % standing", for this and #2 together; the hardware isn't named in that line (their
  bench elsewhere in the commit is NVIDIA: RTX 3060 Ti, driver 616.92).
- **Our tree.** `mesh_indirect_convert` (gpu/vk/vk_draw.cpp:7393) and its ring (`kMeshRing`, `mesh_ring_mu`) are
  their "before" word for word, plus our `PROF_EXACT()` and the `RX_VK_MESH_ARGS` default-off note. Our NOP_BARRIER
  case (vk_replay.cpp:1903) is the same as theirs apart from the barrier-flag census. We don't have it.
- **Hardware.** Every GPU on the native mesh path: NVIDIA Turing+, AMD RDNA2+ on Windows (**the owner's 9070 XT**),
  Intel Arc. Off on the compute fallback (`mesh_ahead_on` checks `dev.mesh_fallback`), so Linux AMD / Steam Deck keep
  today's path. Fewer GPU pass breaks helps GPU-bound PCs most.
- **Port.** Mechanical, ~110 lines: `MeshDraw` + three declarations in vk_internal.h; `mesh_ring_take` split out of
  `mesh_indirect_convert`, the lookup at its top, `mesh_ahead_on` / `mesh_op_set` / `mesh_convert_ahead` in vk_draw.cpp;
  in vk_replay.cpp, the clear at the top of `replay()`, the scan in NOP_BARRIER, `mesh_op_set(p)` in NOP_DRAW_MESH.
  Adapt: our replay names the payload `a`/`avail`; check our op list for ops theirs lacks (an unknown op stops the scan,
  which is safe); our `mesh_indirect_convert` honours `RX_VK_MESH_IND_COUNT_WORD` (their gate turns the feature off
  when it is set: keep that). switches.def row (kill, on). A game-free test for the scan's stop rule (srccut `block`).
- **Risk.** Low-medium: a draw finding stale records would draw the wrong foliage count. The generation and op pointer
  make that unlikely; the ring is 4 MB (frames of draws). Untested with RX_VK_PARALLEL (off by default here): keep it
  off there, or test. Off switch RX_VK_INDIRECT_AHEAD=0. A/B in a foliage area (Kokiri, Lost Woods) and the Market.

### 2. A part ends at the game's own pass end (88e38c0, RX_VK_SPLIT_AT_PASS, on) - PORT FIRST, with #1
- **What it does.** A submission is cut into parts every 120 draws/dispatches (`maybe_split`, RX_VK_CHUNK). The cut
  ended the game's render pass mid-way, and the next part began the same pass again: every target loaded again, a
  second begin, the tiling lost. Now the cut waits for the next NOP_BARRIER or NOP_SET_RT (where the pass ends
  anyway), unless the pass runs on for 4 parts' worth of draws (the forest's 1,200-draw passes), then it cuts as before.
- **Measured.** Part of the "+3.1 % standing" above.
- **Our tree.** Same `maybe_split` / `split_part`, plus our Intel rule `part_ends_before_draw` (RX_VK_SPLIT_BEFORE_DRAW,
  the white-shards fix) and RX_VK_READBACK_EARLY's `early_split`. Keep their order: Intel's before-draw rule returns
  first, so on Intel nothing changes. `early_split` should keep cutting at once (a readback waits for it).
- **Hardware.** Every GPU; most on tile-based or bandwidth-poor ones (iGPUs, Steam Deck, low-end). Neutral-to-good on
  the owner's.
- **Port.** ~15 lines in vk_replay.cpp: `part_end_at_pass`, its check at the top of the replay loop (beside our
  `part_end_wanted` one), the `every * 4` rule in `maybe_split`, the reset in `split_part` and the two places that start
  a recording. switches.def row. Update any check in tests/ that cuts `maybe_split` (run tests/run_all.py).
- **Risk.** Low-medium: parts up to 4x longer means the GPU starts a long pass later (more latency on the CPU-bound
  side, but no more than RX_VK_CHUNK=480 in that pass). RX_VK_SPLIT_AT_PASS=0 off.

### 3. The reaper lets submits and presents go first; capture buffers freed by it (88e38c0, RX_VK_REAP_TURNS, RX_VK_CAP_FREE_HERE) - PORT
- **What it does.** The driver holds one lock for object creation/destruction, memory frees and queue submits/presents
  (seen as a submit waiting while another thread destroys). The reaper (our vk_vma.cpp:350, from ReLink 3168901 via
  Mako 4.1.1) now waits, 3 ms at most, before each of its driver calls while a `DriverTurn` is alive around
  vkQueueSubmit2 / the present's submit. And `cap_buf_free`'s vmaDestroyBuffer (a millisecond and more, under the pools'
  lock, in a burst of captures at an area arrival) goes to the reaper.
- **Measured.** Submits held over 1 ms: 66 -> 37 (their RX_VK_SUBMIT_STATS); NVIDIA by the commit's context.
- **Our tree.** Reaper and `reap()` are theirs; `reap_wait_turn` and `DriverTurn` are missing. Our `cap_buf_free`
  (vk_memory.cpp:1150) destroys inline (Triforce's "with the lock let go" when `cap_spare_fit`); not on the reaper.
- **Hardware.** NVIDIA Windows most (the driver behind ReLink's 3168901 finding), any GPU a little. The owner's AMD
  driver: unknown, likely neutral.
- **Port.** ~45 lines: `driver_turns` + `DriverTurn` in vk_internal.h; `reap_wait_turn` in the reaper loop; a
  `DriverTurn turn;` at our submit sites (vk_replay.cpp ~271, 941, 2532, 6147 and the presents ~6264-6385, including the
  presenter thread's queue); the reaper branch in `cap_buf_free`. Two switches.def rows.
- **Risk.** Low. The reaper only runs later; nothing waits on it. Watch VRAM headroom on 4 GB cards (capture buffers'
  memory returns a little later).

### 4. ASTC->BC3: 4 layers of 1024x1024 a run through an aliased scratch (88e38c0, RX_VK_BC_SMALL_RUNS, NVIDIA only) - PORT (NVIDIA)
- **What it does.** The BC3 scratch is 2048x2048x1 (16 MB); the same memory as 1024x1024x4 through
  `vmaCreateAliasingImage`. Levels of 1024 and less of a texture array (Hyrule Field's 90-layer terrain arrays) decode
  and encode 4 layers a run instead of one: a quarter of the runs, each with three full barriers. The image in use
  starts from UNDEFINED each run (Vulkan's aliasing rule).
- **Measured.** -21 % GPU a decode, on NVIDIA. On by default only there: runs of layers gave AMD's Windows driver dark
  lines on Hyrule Field (the same symptom our own `bc_layers` comment records on an RX 6600).
- **Our tree.** Same scratch (vk_texture.cpp:753 `kBcDim = 2048`, `bc_layers()` 1 by default), same output buffer size
  (4 MB = 4 x 1024^2 blocks), and `bc_encode_into` already takes a `from` view. We lack `create_aliasing_image`.
- **Hardware.** NVIDIA (loads, area arrivals). No effect on AMD/Intel by default; the owner's PC unchanged.
- **Port.** ~80 lines, mechanical: the aliasing helper in vk_vma.cpp; bc_small image/view in `bc_scratch_make`, its
  layout barrier, the run size and the alias barrier in `upload_astc_gpu`; destroy it with the scratch. switches.def row.
- **Risk.** Low on NVIDIA; can't be tested here (no NVIDIA card): verify-list item.

### 5. XInput polled on a thread of its own (550bde6, HOS_XINPUT_INLINE=1 off) - PORT (small)
- **What it does.** An empty XInput slot is slow to ask (0.05-0.1 ms on their bench, a millisecond or more on PCs with
  Bluetooth or a virtual-pad driver), and the four empty slots were asked together once a second on the game's thread.
  A thread (above-normal priority) asks them: connected slots every 2 ms, empty ones once a second; the game reads the
  last answers under an SRW lock.
- **Measured.** Empty slots cost the game's thread 0.21-0.31 ms a second.
- **Our tree.** `pads_read` (host/nn_hid.c ~760-850) still asks on the game's thread, with the same 1 s retry per empty
  slot. Ours polls XInput only when SDL has no pads (or HOS_XINPUT=1): exactly the keyboard player's case relink
  targets. We load from System32 (`LoadLibraryExA ... SEARCH_SYSTEM32`): keep that in the moved loader. Our loop also
  keeps `xinput_pads_now`, the dead-zone/invert options (`rx_option(11/16/17)`): adapt.
- **Hardware.** Keyboard players and XInput-only pads, laptops with Bluetooth. Windows only.
- **Port.** ~70 lines in nn_hid.c, a switches.def row (HOS_XINPUT_INLINE). Keep the SDL gate: the thread starts only
  when XInput would be polled.
- **Risk.** Low. A pad state up to 2 ms old (the game reads at 60 Hz).

### 6. ASTC alpha scan (88e38c0, RX_VK_ASTC_ALPHA_SCAN, diagnostic) - LATER (idea for low VRAM)
- Counts BC3-kept textures whose every ASTC block has only L/RGB endpoint modes (or an opaque void extent): those could
  be BC1, half the VRAM. Cheap to copy as a diagnostic first and run once in a tour; a BC1 path would follow only if
  the share is large. Helps 4-6 GB cards and iGPUs.

### 7. "vk: late shaders" report (88e38c0) - LATER (diagnosis)
- Every 10 s while any came late: draws left out for their program vs their pipeline, pipelines made at a draw (queue
  and build times), programs waited for. Our census (vk_programs.cpp, RX_VK_LOG_PROGRAM_LIFE) covers much of this, but
  not on by default. Worth it only if players' logs keep failing to tell program from pipeline stalls.

## Excluded (touches the lift / a randomizer file)
- **550bde6 OptimizeChangeBank hold (HOS_OCB_HOLD_MS, host/oot_ocb.c + `patch_ocb` in nx2/oot_patches.py).** OoT's
  neighbouring-area manager (f_832b9880) flips its pick when Link stands where two areas' radii overlap (the Market's
  plaza), throwing away a half-made area each flip. The hold keeps a flip *back* to an area let go less than 10 s ago
  for 1.5 s. Measured: Market edge area reloads -73 %, time lost in hitches 12-29 % lower in four pairs. Helps few-core
  CPUs most. **Not ported:** it patches the lift through nx2/oot_patches.py. Worth naming to the owner as an idea (our
  hos_thread.c:1114 already treats the OptimizeChange thread specially, and host/oot_heap_retry.c is already hooked
  there).

## Already ours, or not applicable
- **Texel reads/writes give up the virtual core** (550bde6): ours since Longshot 1780fbe0 / HeyListen 77bd2f86
  (host/rx_backend.c:571-603, HOS_TEXELS_KEEP_CORE=1 off).
- **Graphics pipelines made ahead, now off** (RX_VK_EAGER_GRAPHICS): we never had it. Their numbers (77 % built for
  nothing, 463 s of builds where the driver keeps no cache) are a reason never to add it.
- **Programs read ahead at set** (RX_VK_PROG_AHEAD): ours loads kept programs at their create calls by default
  (load_default.h `choose`, RX_VK_LOAD=create), and program_prefetch parses stages at set (vk_shader.cpp:2946).
- **Compute modules let go after their pipelines** (RX_VK_KEEP_MODULES): ours lets go of every ready program's modules
  after the precompile (`program_modules_release`, vk_draw.cpp:10547), more than theirs.
- **Texture decoders' pipelines before the game**: ours, `texture_pipelines_warm` (vk_texture.cpp:1364).
- **No-mesh fallback: vertex past the count, task-started slots only**: our fallback is a different design that already
  does both (records taken in order, the draw's count is the records written; the mesh dispatch reads the count of
  started slots from the records' header, vk_draw.cpp ~7758). Nothing to port.
- **GPL rework** (shader libraries per program, linked at the draw, RX_VK_GPL_FULL): opt-in on both sides; they measured
  linked pipelines 8-20 % slower on the GPU. Skip until GPL is wanted here.
- **HOS_SHADOW_BUCKET_ALL** (opt-in, all square depth formats and small sides): our shadow_slot.h rule is their old
  one. Unmeasured, opt-in: skip.
- **RX_VK_SUBMIT_STATS, RX_VK_MODULE_COST, RX_VK_PASS_TIMING fallback names**: diagnostics, skip.
