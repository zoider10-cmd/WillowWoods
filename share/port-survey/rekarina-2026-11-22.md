## Summary

Rekarina is a separate recompilation (LAS Recomp lifter + MacNativeGames' TotK runtime, an NVN-command replay onto its
own Vulkan back end, Ryujinx's shader translator with 45 patches), developed on an RTX 5070; AMD is known-weak there
(RX 7000/9000 sunlight flicker, device losses and "crashes that take the PC down" are still open in 1.2.10). So almost
nothing in its renderer is portable or needed on our RX 9070 XT: our renderer is a different design and already ahead
on AMD. The game-side work is where it pays: its notes say it read Hookshot's PRs for the game's facts, and most of its
above-60-fps patch sets match ones we already have. **Address check:** their `main+X` is our `f_(X+0x80020000)` for
every place I compared (the day clock 0x3cdf70 = our f_803edf70, the sound job 0x415300 = f_80435300, the steering
update 0x261e8e0 = f_8263e8e0, the render-target pool allocator 0x3ed00 = f_8005ed00, the nn::diag abort 0x34fc280 =
f_8351c280, the global step 0x3b4ea90 = f_83b6ea90), so their sites can be looked up directly in our lift, not re-found
from scratch. New to us: a fix for the game's lock-free queues aborting (a crash after the first boss), a null-resource
guard, the frozen-pose fix extended below 60 fps at a 60 cap, an AMD pipeline-cache size limit, the dash without a
roll, a disk texture cache, and an automatic save import. No randomizer support in it.

## Candidates, best first

### 1. `ringpost`: the game's lock-free queues abort instead of dropping a record (crash) - **worth porting first** (small)
- **What:** the game's "post a named text value" (their main+0xd24e50, ours f_80d44e50) writes a record into a lock-free
  ring that the consumer (main+0xb99980, ours f_80bb9980) drains and swaps once a frame. A writer still copying at the
  swap, or one that finds the ring full (96 records), tail-branches to the nn::diag abort (ours f_8351c280: we end with
  `hos_fatal "the game aborted"`). On the console the timing keeps writers clear; on a PC a worker can be held up. A
  player's game ended after the first boss's death this way. The set turns each such `b <abort>` into `ret`: the
  record is dropped or read a frame later. The same writer is compiled into 77 more functions (the game's other
  lock-free queues; each commits with mask 0xf00fffff and tail-branches to the abort): 78 sites.
- **Where:** `recomp/patches-oot-v111.txt` set `ringpost` (1.2.6, "a crash when one of the game's internal queues
  filled up", notes 2026-11-20 "The game's lock-free queues"). Default on. `REKARINA_RING_LOG=1` samples the ring (at
  most 7 of 96 in use, ~19 swaps a second in the Market, field, castle).
- **Gain:** a crash fix; unmeasured beyond one player report (8-thread CPU).
- **Ours:** not there (no ring guard in nx2/oot_patches.py; AbortImpl is fatal in host/nn_diag.c). Our virtual cores
  may make a held-up writer rarer, but nothing proves it can't happen.
- **Hardware:** any; most likely on CPUs with few threads.
- **Port:** a lift patch in nx2/oot_patches.py: in each listed function, the tail `b f_8351c280` -> return. Take their
  78 site list (add 0x80020000) and check each is the ring writer's tail (mask 0xf00fffff before it), NOT one of the
  other callers of f_8351c280 (our heap-budget abort in f_83b7b340 also goes there: leave those). Doesn't touch the
  randomizer's patches, but it is the lift. Test: a lift-only check that the sites patched count is 78; no game test
  can force the race.
- **Risk:** low: only the path that would have aborted changes; a dropped record is a lost UI text value for a frame.

### 2. AMD's pipeline cache limit (~850 MiB) - **check first** (no code until checked)
- **What:** their notes (`runtime/vk_pipeline.c`, 1.2.9): "AMD's driver keeps a pipeline cache of at most about 850 MiB";
  a file filled past that "took nothing new, and every start built the entry step's pipelines again (6,500 of them,
  four minutes on an RX 6800)". Their fix: on AMD a cache file >= 768 MiB is started empty once
  (`REKARINA_PIPELINE_CACHE_RESET_MB`), and stage libraries stay out of it.
- **Ours:** we hand AMD's driver a ~1.2 GB `cache/hookshot_vk_pipelines.bin` (gpu/vk/pcache_load.h; 1.19 GB on the
  owner's PC per the Reborn survey). `RX_VK_COLD_CHECK` reruns the warm pass when the driver "lost" noted pipelines.
- **Check (cheap):** in the owner's log, does the cold check say "it lost them" at most starts, and does the cache file
  stop growing near 850 MiB of driver data / does a pipeline seen last session compile again in play? If so, the
  owner's first minutes each session carry pipeline builds: a 1 % low cost. Then: keep the AMD file under the limit
  (leave stage libraries out, or start fresh past a size), like theirs.
- **Hardware:** AMD Windows driver only. **Risk:** low; no game behaviour.

### 3. Frozen poses below 60 fps at a cap of 60 (`looppose` on any fractional step) - **later** (tiny)
- **What:** 1.2.9/1.2.10 (notes 2026-11-21 "the frozen figures under the ice at 48 fps"): the one-frame animation loops
  (the frozen Zoras) twitch at any fractional step, also 1.25 at 48 fps; their fix acts on every fractional step,
  whole steps (60, 30 fps) unchanged. Their notes: "Another port's fix of the same twitch (above 60 fps only) leaves
  it under 60 as well" - that is ours.
- **Ours:** host/nn_vi.c hos_loop_step / hos_loop_pose act only while `hos_frame_seconds_bits != 0` (a cap above 60).
  At the owner's 120 cap a 48 fps frame is already handled (loop_step carries any step, loop_pose holds the start).
  Only a player at a 60 cap who drops below 60 still sees it (real-time steps of 1.25 etc. come from realtime_rate).
- **Port:** in host/nn_vi.c, treat a step with a fraction as `timed` for these two (host/loop_step.h's tests in
  tests/ cover the logic). No lift change, no randomizer code. **Risk:** low. Same idea would apply to the whole-frame
  counters (day clock at 48 fps adds (int)2.5 = 2 s a frame: 80 % speed), which they do NOT fix either.

### 4. The render-target heap in the room beside the Market's gate - **check first**
- **What:** 1.2.6: walking into the room of pots and torches beside the Market's gate froze the game: the game asks for
  6-17 square shadow maps a frame there; its first-fit render-target heap (305 MiB, the pool our f_8005ed00 allocates
  from) ran out, the retry read a null (their fault handler looped on it). Theirs failed at 305 and 384 MiB, played at
  448+; fix: +256 MiB for 16:9.
- **Ours:** nx2/oot_patches.py grows that same pool (0x13100000) with the output's pixels above 2560x1440 only
  (`hos_rt_pool_size`; we met the same null at 4K). At 1440p or below it stays 305 MiB. Their storage sizes are their
  own (padded square depth targets), so ours may fit; unknown.
- **Check:** a Go there / walk into that room (Market entrance guard house) at the owner's output, camera moving; a
  crash or hang in f_8005ed00 means grow the pool below 4K too (the existing patch, a larger minimum). Touches the
  lift only if needed.

### 5. `resguard`: a null resource entry looked up by handle - **later** (only if seen)
- **What:** their main+0xc938d0 (ours f_80cb38d0) resource lookup by handle: a negative id, one past the table or an
  empty entry read the entry's +0x150 at address 0x150. Seen rarely in the forest at any frame rate. Three branches
  (c93918 tbnz, c93924 b.le, c93930 cbz) go to the function's own invalid-handle path (null out, which callers test).
- **Ours:** not there. A crash log with a read of guest 0x150 in f_80cb38d0 would be it. Tiny lift patch (3 branches).
  **Risk:** low (the game's own invalid path). Keep in mind with the Hookshot #2451 OptimizeChangeBank one (which we
  have: host/oot_heap_retry.c).

### 6. Dash on its own input, without the roll (`dash`) - **later** (feature)
- **What:** 1.2.1 added a dash on the left stick's press (or V); 1.2.10 makes it start at once without the roll A's
  press gives: the hero dashes while bit 4 of his input flags (*(hero+0x50)) is set; two reads (their main+0x4ae798 in
  the movement update main+0x4aaa80, main+0xc07f3c in the stance/camera update main+0xc025d0; ours f_804caa80 and
  f_80c225d0) get bit 4 ORed in while the dash input is held, for the player's hero only. No memory written. Credited
  to NX2navi "for showing how".
- **Ours:** no dash input. **Port:** two lift sites + an input binding (host/nn_hid.c, the menu's Controls). Not the
  randomizer's code. Player value: moderate (a PC convenience). **Risk:** low-moderate (the hero's state machine).

### 7. A texture cache on disk (ASTC converted to BC3 once) - **later, measure first** (large)
- **What:** `tex_cache` (default on since 1.2.0): the BC3 blocks a GPU ASTC decode+encode makes are written to
  `tex-cache/<storage hash>-<format>-<size>-<levels>x<layers>.bc3` (up to 14 GB), and a later texture with that storage
  hash is one buffer-to-image copy; the setup can fill it ahead ("Prepare textures", ~12 GB); a worker-thread "texture
  stream" reads files before first use. Opaque textures also go to BC1 (half size).
- **Gain (theirs, RTX 5070, running through the field, second minute):** 72.8 fps / 1 % low 36.0 / 58 frames over
  25 ms -> 84.5 / 55.6 / 5 with the cache filled; the village's first visit 53 -> 96 fps with textures prepared.
  Their cost was the GPU decode inside the frame that first draws the texture; first reads of freshly written files
  were slow (200 MB/s), and it shook out a device loss in their texture pool.
- **Ours:** no disk cache; ASTC is decoded on the GPU (BC3 for big ones, RX_VK_ASTC_BC_*), with ASTC reuse by GPU copy
  and the texture tiles/compaction from Longshot. Our memory notes say the owner's Market lows are game CPU work, not
  the renderer. **First:** see whether ASTC decode/encode shows in our long frames on area arrival / running through
  Hyrule Field (the renderer's section timings). Only then: an idea port into gpu/vk (vk_texture.cpp), not their code.
- **Risk:** moderate-high (stale texels if the hash misses a write; disk space; a first-read slowness).

### 8. Save import that finds the saves itself - **later** (launcher only)
- **What:** `installer/saves.py`: the launcher's Saves page lists saves found in Ryujinx and its forks (Ryubing), the
  yuzu family (yuzu, suyu, eden, citron, sudachi, torzu) and other ports' install folders (HookshotBuilder,
  LongshotLauncher), or a folder or a .zip; checks it is this game's save by its files (slot_NN with progress.sav and
  caption.sav); moves the current save to `save-before-import/<time>/` and can restore it. Tests in saves_test.py.
- **Ours:** launcher/Launcher.cs OnImport opens a "SAVE FOLDER" and asks the player to drop the folder in by hand; the
  game copies it at the next start with a backup. **Port:** C# in the launcher, the search list as data. Doesn't touch
  the randomizer, but an imported save meets the randomizer's seed check ("Use this save"). **Risk:** low.

### 9. Full-screen exclusive disallowed on the plain swapchain - **later** (small)
- **What:** 1.2.9 (notes 2026-11-21): with V-Sync FIFO or immediate, NVIDIA and AMD's Windows drivers may take a
  borderless window covering the monitor into full-screen exclusive (a flash at each switch, oversaturated colours,
  odd Alt+Tab; mailbox is composed and fine). They chain `VkSurfaceFullScreenExclusiveInfoEXT` =
  `DISALLOWED` where `VK_EXT_full_screen_exclusive` exists (needs `VK_KHR_get_surface_capabilities2`), retry without
  it on refusal, `REKARINA_VK_FSE=0` to undo. Measured no cost (mailbox, Market, 1 % low within noise). Not confirmed
  on the reporter's PC.
- **Ours:** gpu/vk/vk_main.cpp picks FIFO for V-Sync On, immediate for Off; no FSE info chained. **Port:** small, in
  vk_main.cpp swapchain creation, a new RX_ switch row. Only matters if the owner or players see flashes on V-Sync
  changes. **Risk:** low.

### 10. Small player features we lack - **later**
- **Every cutscene skippable**, including the three the game marks unskippable (the forest opening, the staff credits
  Dm_StaffCredit_0005, the title Dm_Title_0005): the romfs file
  `Event/EventSetting/EventSettingComponent/SkipSetting/DisableSkip.engine__event__SkipSetting.bgyml` served with
  false in every overlay folder (`tools/make_oot_prompts.py`). Our randomizer's Skip cutscenes covers the opening; the
  credits at a seed's end could use it. Romfs-only.
- **Dialogue without the pauses between phrases** (`fast_text`): the message archives rebuilt without the pause tags
  (group 5, types 0-2). Ours has Text speed (letter speed) only. Would interact with our reworded texts (HOS_TEXTS).
- **Mouse camera where the direct path can't hold the angle** (swimming): their stick fallback goes through the inverse
  of the game's stick curve so it turns as fast as the direct path (`input_mouse_stick_curved`). Ours falls back to
  plain stick emulation (host/mouse_native.c), likely slower there. Small, host only.
- **FSR 1 (EASU + RCAS) on the 3D scene** for built-in graphics and 4 GB cards: none here; no use on the owner's card.

## Skipped

- Their whole replay/renderer (NVN command replay, mirrors and write-back merges, late sums, texture pool slots, VMA
  blocks, recorder threads): another design; ours does not share these bugs.
- NonUniform decoration for texture arrays: they turned it off on AMD in 1.2.9.1 (it crashed an RX 6600's driver) and it
  did not cure the RDNA 3/4 flicker. Our Eden-based translator emits NonUniform itself.
- AMD colour images placed past their storage (`color_tail_refused`), mesh-shader emulation for GPUs without mesh
  shaders (translator patch 0045), quad-shuffle helper lanes: their own memory model/translator; RDNA 1/2 or
  pre-mesh cards.
- The exposure step after closing the pause menu (their late-sum timing), the light flicker fixes, frame generation's
  HUD-less picture size: renderer-specific.
- `dofscale` (depth of field at 1280x720): off by default in their own tree (a black quarter of the far scene).
- DLSS-G fallback to FSR FG on a non-NVIDIA card, Streamline: NVIDIA plumbing.
- RenHDrum DualSense haptics: Longshot's integration, already skipped in our Longshot surveys.
- Fewer-than-six-thread CPU tuning, the AVX2-less build, Linux/Proton memory path, the 16 GB setup throttling: not the
  owner's hardware; our renderer and lift differ.
- Atmosphère cheats (code cheats rebuilt as patch sets): no randomizer use; our Debug tab covers testing.
- Their uninstaller, problem-report zip, setup and update machinery: ours has its own launcher flow.

## Randomizer and game flags

No randomizer, no seed or item-placement code. What may help ours:
- `tools/oot_progress.py` documents progress.sav (MurmurHash3 x86-32 names, typed sections split by hash-0 entries,
  Bool 0 ... Bool64bitKey 32 in TotK's order; `PouchData/<actor>`, `IsGet/<actor>`, `PlayerStatus` members
  Life/MaxLife/MP/MPMax/rupees 0xc995bbb8/Equip*, `AssignableItem/Adult|Child`, `Step_<quest>` enums,
  `Sequence_IsChildhood`, maps `<map>_Child|_Adult`). Our rando-test tools already read the same format.
- Time of day: the world clock keeps seconds since midnight at +0x28 (day count +0x2c; matches our HOS_OOT_CLOCK_LOG
  reading), and a **town's** time does not run: at its load the game picks 06:00, 20:00 or 00:00 from two game-data
  bools by hash, 0x804aec99 (clear: 06:00) and 0x8592aa82 (with the first set: 20:00, else 00:00), in their
  main+0x345c450 (ours f_8347c450), times from the table at main+0x3c834e8. Their `REKARINA_DAYTIME=HH:MM` test knob
  holds the clock and sets those bools so a warp into a town arrives at night: a model for a "night" test switch for
  Gold Skulltula / Bombchu Shop night checks (bugs 81, 82).
